## Skills you'll need (section 03)

| Fact used | Source |
|---|---|
| Skills (this experience): Basic samba stepping and stamina to keep moving for the whole parade; Wearing and carrying a full fantasia (costume) through a long Sambadrome parade; Singing along to your school's samba-enredo and following your ala (wing) leaders | Skills research, checked 2026-10-04 (IST) |
| Difficulty (our estimate): Beginner (physically demanding); prep time (our estimate): 3–6 weeks of light stamina practice plus learning the song. Difficulty and prep time are Curiosta editorial estimates for a first-timer, not organiser figures. | Curiosta editorial estimate |
| Refresher, practice and safety steps: To parade, you buy a costume for an ala (wing) of a samba school; the costume is your entry. Schools publish costumes once the year's theme is out. / Learn the samba-enredo (the school's theme song) for your year; singing along matters to the judges' impression of your wing. / Practise a simple samba step you can keep up with: a light, bouncy three-step weight change on the balls of the feet. You don't need to be a dancer, but you must keep moving forward. / Wear the costume exactly as issued, with no extra bags or visible personal items; follow your ala leader's instructions on timing and spacing. / Arrive early at the concentration area, hydrate, and eat before; parades run late at night in heat and humidity. / Behind-the-scenes tours at Cidade do Samba (promoted by Visit Brasil) let you see costume and float workshops and try a few steps. / Learn your school's samba-enredo lyrics from its official channels. / Do 30–45 minutes of continuous light samba stepping to music several times a week before Carnival. / Practise moving in a heavy or bulky costume, e.g. with a weighted backpack and headgear. / Heat and dehydration: drink water before and after. / Break in comfortable footwear that suits your costume. / Keep valuables out of the parade; no bags allowed in wings. | Our summary of the resources below |
