| Skills (this experience): Reading the stages of a Kumartuli clay idol (frame, clay layers, painting, eye-painting on Mahalaya); Respectful behaviour and photography in working artisan studios; Pandal-hopping: moving safely through Durga Puja crowds | Skills research, checked 2026-10-04 (IST) |
| Difficulty (our estimate): Beginner; prep time (our estimate): 1 hour reading and route planning. Difficulty and prep time are Curiosta editorial estimates for a first-timer, not organiser figures. | Curiosta editorial estimate |
| Refresher, practice and safety steps: Kumartuli is a working quarter: artisans sculpt Durga and her family from unfired river clay in the months before the festival. You are a guest in their workplace. / Learn the sequence so you can follow what you see: a frame, clay applied in layers, drying, painting, then the eyes painted on Mahalaya to 'bring the goddess to life'. / Ask before photographing artisans or entering a studio; don't touch drying idols; keep lanes clear for workers carrying heavy pieces. / Buying a small clay piece directly from an artisan is the best way to take part. / During Puja, plan pandal routes, go early or late, and agree a meeting point with your group in case you get separated. / On immersion days, watch from designated spots; riverbanks get crowded and slippery. / Read the UNESCO description so you know what you are looking at. / Model a small clay figure at home to understand layering and drying. / Pick 5–6 pandals on a map in advance and plan a walking route. / Ask before photographing people; don't touch idols. / Crowds peak at night during Puja; agree a meeting point. / Take care on riverbanks on immersion days. | Our summary of the resources below |
