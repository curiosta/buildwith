03 · Skills you'll need

Skills you'll need

What to learn and practise before you go. Each resource is marked Official or Community-accepted and names its publisher. Difficulty and prep time are our estimate.

Core skill

Preparing a maker project for public exhibition (working demo, safety, power needs)

Also useful:

  • Writing a strong Call for Makers application
  • Running an interactive stand and explaining your project to visitors
Difficulty our estimateSome practice (you need a working project)
Prep time our estimate2–6 weeks to polish an existing project for exhibition

Difficulty and prep time are Curiosta editorial estimates for a first-timer, not organiser figures.

Refresher

  1. Read the Call for Makers regulations: projects are selected partly on novelty and also on technical, logistical and safety requirements for the in-person event.
  2. Write the application around what visitors will see and do: a clear photo or video, what's new about it, and the space and power you need.
  3. Make the exhibit safe to stand next to: guard moving parts, hot surfaces and batteries; tidy cables; plan for crowds and children.
  4. Make the stand self-explanatory: signs, process photos and something people can touch or try.
  5. Stand in front of your table, greet people, and have a 30-second story ready: why you built it, what you learned, what's next.
  6. Plan set-up and take-down, spares and a repair kit; demos break.

Practise before you go

  • Demo your project to friends or at a local makerspace or college fest and note the questions they ask.
  • Write your 30-second pitch and a one-page project sign.
  • Run your project for 6–8 hours straight to find failure points.

Safety notes

  • Guard moving parts, hot surfaces and battery packs; follow organiser electrical rules.
  • Keep walkways clear of cables.
