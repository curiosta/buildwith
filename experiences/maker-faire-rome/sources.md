## Skills you'll need (section 03)

| Fact used | Source |
|---|---|
| Skills (this experience): Preparing a maker project for public exhibition (working demo, safety, power needs); Writing a strong Call for Makers application; Running an interactive stand and explaining your project to visitors | Skills research, checked 2026-10-04 (IST) |
| Difficulty (our estimate): Some practice (you need a working project); prep time (our estimate): 2–6 weeks to polish an existing project for exhibition. Difficulty and prep time are Curiosta editorial estimates for a first-timer, not organiser figures. | Curiosta editorial estimate |
| Refresher, practice and safety steps: Read the Call for Makers regulations: projects are selected partly on novelty and also on technical, logistical and safety requirements for the in-person event. / Write the application around what visitors will see and do: a clear photo or video, what's new about it, and the space and power you need. / Make the exhibit safe to stand next to: guard moving parts, hot surfaces and batteries; tidy cables; plan for crowds and children. / Make the stand self-explanatory: signs, process photos and something people can touch or try. / Stand in front of your table, greet people, and have a 30-second story ready: why you built it, what you learned, what's next. / Plan set-up and take-down, spares and a repair kit; demos break. / Demo your project to friends or at a local makerspace or college fest and note the questions they ask. / Write your 30-second pitch and a one-page project sign. / Run your project for 6–8 hours straight to find failure points. / Guard moving parts, hot surfaces and battery packs; follow organiser electrical rules. / Keep walkways clear of cables. | Our summary of the resources below |
