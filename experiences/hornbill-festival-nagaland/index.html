03 · Skills you'll need

Skills you'll need

What to learn and practise before you go. Each resource is marked Official or Community-accepted and names its publisher. Difficulty and prep time are our estimate.

Core skill

Recognising Naga craft forms: bamboo and cane, loin-loom weaving, beadwork, wood carving

Also useful:

  • Buying respectfully from artisan stalls and craft demonstrations
  • Permits and logistics: Inner Line Permit (Indian visitors) or Protected Area Permit (foreign visitors)
Difficulty our estimateBeginner
Prep time our estimate1 hour reading plus permit application lead time

Difficulty and prep time are Curiosta editorial estimates for a first-timer, not organiser figures.

Refresher

  1. Hornbill showcases the work of all Naga tribes at Kisama; crafts include handwoven textiles, bamboo and cane, beadwork, wood carving and paintings.
  2. Each tribe's motifs and shawls carry meaning; ask the maker about the pattern before buying and treat ceremonial items with respect.
  3. Ask before photographing performers, morung interiors or people in traditional dress.
  4. Indian visitors from outside Nagaland need an Inner Line Permit (apply online); foreign nationals follow the Protected Area Permit route. Apply well before December.
  5. A day-wise event list is released by the government shortly before the festival, so check it for craft demonstrations.

Practise before you go

  • Read the official events page and shortlist craft demonstrations and the bamboo carnival.
  • Apply for your ILP online in advance and keep a printed copy.
  • Learn the names of a few tribes and their textile traditions so conversations with artisans go further.

Safety notes

  • Carry your ILP/PAP at all times in Nagaland.
  • Ask before photographing people or ceremonies.
  • December evenings at Kisama are cold; dress in layers.
