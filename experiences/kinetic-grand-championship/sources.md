| Skills (this experience): Designing a human-powered amphibious sculpture that crosses roads, sand, mud and open water; Flotation, traction and drivetrain design (pedal power on land and water); Following the 'Offishul Rules', including ACE status, self-sufficiency and safe conduct | Skills research, checked 2026-10-04 (IST) |
| Difficulty (our estimate): Skilled team; prep time (our estimate): 3–6 months of design, build and water testing. Difficulty and prep time are Curiosta editorial estimates for a first-timer, not organiser figures. | Curiosta editorial estimate |
| Refresher, practice and safety steps: All forward movement must be human-powered over the whole course; for ACE status no batteries or stored energy may drive forward movement, and tyre or pontoon inflation must use human-powered pumps. / Everything needed for normal operation (flotation, water propulsion, pumps, sand or mud traction aids, tools) must be carried on the sculpture all the way. / Design for three terrains: wide tyres or traction aids for sand and mud, gearing for hills, and flotation plus paddle wheels or propellers for water. / Safety rules prohibit projectiles, anchors, explosives and fireworks; fuel, compressed gas and lithium batteries must follow best practice (the rules point to Burning Man's flame-effects guidance). / Read the engineering and art judging criteria early; the race rewards art and engineering as well as speed. / Team roles: captain, pilots, pit crew; everyone signs the waiver and code of conduct. / Read 'How to Build a Kinetic' and sketch your drivetrain and flotation. / Test flotation of your pontoon design in a pool or lake with the full pilot load. / Build stamina with long cycling rides, including sand or mud if possible. / Flotation must carry the full pilot load; test before race day. / No projectiles, anchors, fireworks or explosives; handle fuels and lithium batteries to best practice. / All team members sign the waiver and code of conduct. | Our summary of the resources below |
