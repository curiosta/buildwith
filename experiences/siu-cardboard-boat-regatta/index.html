01 · How to take part

Your way in

Build a cardboard boat at home and register on race morning, or build an instant boat on site from a kit (first come, first served); paddles and life jackets are provided.

Team entry

Build a cardboard boat and race it

Build a muscle-powered boat from corrugated cardboard (the hull must be cardboard; one-part waterproofing only), then register and pass inspection on race morning. Races run on a U-shaped course of about 300 yards; up to 10 people per boat.

Workshop / open build

Or build an instant boat on the day

Instant-boat kits (cardboard, a box knife and tape) are handed out at Campus Lake on a first-come, first-served basis; you get about two hours to build. Paddles and life jackets are provided.

03 · Skills you'll need

Skills you'll need

What to learn and practise before you go. Each resource is marked Official or Community-accepted and names its publisher. Difficulty and prep time are our estimate.

Core skill

Cardboard boat building: hull shape, layered corrugated sheets, glued and taped seams

Also useful:

  • Waterproofing with a one-part coating (no epoxy or fibreglass)
  • Paddling as a crew and getting out safely if the boat sinks
Difficulty our estimateBeginner
Prep time our estimate2–3 weekend build sessions (or none with an instant-boat kit)

Difficulty and prep time are Curiosta editorial estimates for a first-timer, not organiser figures.

Refresher

  1. Everything structural must be corrugated cardboard, including the hull, superstructure and seats.
  2. Glue, caulk, tape and staples are allowed as secondary supplies; waterproof the hull with a one-part product only.
  3. Design for a quick exit: every crew member must be able to get out easily if the boat sinks.
  4. Boats are muscle-powered from inside the boat; no swimming or pushing from outside.
  5. The course is a U shape of about 300 yards: round each buoy on the outside.
  6. Life jackets and paddles are provided and must be worn and used; wear water shoes.

Practise before you go

  • Build a small test hull from 2–3 sheets and float it in a pool or bath before the full build.
  • Practise paddling in rhythm with your crew.
  • If you can't build at home, plan on the instant-boat kit: about two hours to build on the day.

Safety notes

  • Wear the life jacket provided at all times on the water.
  • Design for an easy exit if the boat sinks.
  • Knives and box cutters: cut away from yourself.
