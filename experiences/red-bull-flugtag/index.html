03 · Skills you'll need

Skills you'll need

What to learn and practise before you go. Each resource is marked Official or Community-accepted and names its publisher. Difficulty and prep time are our estimate.

Core skill

Designing a light, floating, human-powered 'flying machine' to the size and weight limits

Also useful:

  • Choosing water-safe materials (closed-cell foam, wood) and a strong but light structure
  • Pilot water safety and a 30-second showmanship routine
Difficulty our estimateSkilled team
Prep time our estimate2–3 months of team build after selection

Difficulty and prep time are Curiosta editorial estimates for a first-timer, not organiser figures.

Refresher

  1. Use the current event's rules: in 2026 the Minneapolis and Miami rules cap the craft at 28 ft wingtip to wingtip and 20 ft nose to tail, and craft plus pilot at 400 lb (the FAQ quotes under 22 ft wide), with a team of five: one pilot plus four ground crew.
  2. Human power only: no engines, catapults or stored energy, and no adapted hang-gliders or light aircraft.
  3. It must float and be recoverable: choose materials lighter than water that don't waterlog (closed-cell foam, wood) and keep the weight down so it gets up the ramp.
  4. Avoid prohibited items such as styrofoam and caster wheels (official rules guide); cover any chicken wire.
  5. Everyone who jumps must be able to swim 100 yards unaided, in costume. Helmets and life jackets are worn for launch.
  6. Score comes from distance, creativity and showmanship; plan the skit as carefully as the wings.

Practise before you go

  • Build a scale glider from foam board to test wing area and balance.
  • Swim 100 yards in clothes to check you can meet the rule.
  • Rehearse the 30-second routine with your crew.

Safety notes

  • All jumpers must swim 100 yards unaided in costume.
  • Helmets and life jackets for launch.
  • No catapults or stored energy; craft must float.
