| Refresher, practice and safety steps: A good kite is light but strong: straight, balanced spars, a taut sail and a correctly placed bridle point matter more than decoration. / Tie the bridle so the kite meets the wind at a slight angle; move the tow point up in stronger wind and down in lighter wind, in small steps. / Add a lightweight tail if the kite spins or dives in gusts. / Launch with your back to the wind; let line out and pull in as it climbs. A helper can do a long launch in light wind. / On a busy family field, keep clear of show-kite arenas and other people's lines; wear a hat and sunscreen. / Cape Mental Health's festival includes kite-making workshops; book or join on the day as the programme allows. / Build a sled or diamond kite from a plastic bag and two sticks following AKA's kite-making pointers. / Test-fly it at a park and adjust bridle and tail. / Bring your own decorated kite to fly if workshop places fill up. / Keep clear of show-kite arenas and lines. / Sun protection; gloves if your kite pulls hard. | Our summary of the resources below |
