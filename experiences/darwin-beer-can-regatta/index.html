03 · Skills you'll need

Skills you'll need

What to learn and practise before you go. Each resource is marked Official or Community-accepted and names its publisher. Difficulty and prep time are our estimate.

Core skill

Building a boat whose flotation comes from sealed drink cans, cartons or bottles

Also useful:

  • Structure: can 'strings', chicken-wire bundling, deck and chine; catamaran layouts for big crews
  • Water safety and race-day rules (flotation jackets, tow rope)
Difficulty our estimateSome practice (team build)
Prep time our estimate3–6 weekends of can collection and building

Difficulty and prep time are Curiosta editorial estimates for a first-timer, not organiser figures.

Refresher

  1. Seal every container: tape cans in pairs open end to open end, and seal with tape or glue, because each sealed can is a little float.
  2. Tape cans into straight 'strings' 1–2 m long (laying them in a length of angle iron keeps them straight), then bundle the strings together with chicken wire.
  3. Add a deck to sit or stand on and a chine (a sharp bend along the hull) for shape; big rowing crews use a catamaran layout.
  4. The craft should look as if it's made from recycled drink containers; any design is allowed if the committee judges it safe.
  5. Fit a tow rope no longer than the craft to the bow, and every crew member wears a flotation jacket.
  6. Register every person aboard by 5 pm the day before; assemble 15 minutes before your event; remove your boat afterwards (littering is penalised).

Practise before you go

  • Collect and seal cans weeks ahead; test a 1 m can string's buoyancy in a bucket or pool.
  • Build a small test raft and load it with a person in a pool or calm water with supervision.
  • Practise paddling in sync as a crew.

Safety notes

  • Every crew member wears a flotation jacket.
  • Tow rope on the bow; boat must be judged safe by the committee.
  • Sun and heat at Mindil Beach; follow water-safety officials and local beach signage.
