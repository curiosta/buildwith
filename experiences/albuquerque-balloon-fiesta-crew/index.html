03 · Skills you'll need

Skills you'll need

What to learn and practise before you go. Each resource is marked Official or Community-accepted and names its publisher. Difficulty and prep time are our estimate.

Core skill

Ground-crewing a hot-air balloon: layout, cold and hot inflation (holding the mouth, crown line), launch

Also useful:

  • Chase and retrieval: tracking the balloon, landowner courtesy, pack-down
  • Crew safety: gloves, clothing, never wrapping lines around hands
Difficulty our estimateBeginner (physical, early starts)
Prep time our estimate1–2 hours reading

Difficulty and prep time are Curiosta editorial estimates for a first-timer, not organiser figures.

Refresher

  1. Chase crews help the pilot inflate, launch, chase, deflate and pack the balloon. You register online in advance or sign up at the Chase Crew tower on the field.
  2. Listen to the pilot's crew briefing: every pilot assigns roles (crown line, holding the mouth, basket) and you do only your role.
  3. Wear smooth leather gloves (loose and easy to remove), natural-fibre layers, sturdy closed shoes, a hat and eye protection. Mornings are cold and the sun is strong at altitude.
  4. Never wrap a rope or line around your hand or body; if the balloon lifts, let go.
  5. During chase, keep the balloon in sight, obey traffic laws, and never drive onto private land without permission; respect crops and livestock.
  6. At pack-down, squeeze air out from the crown towards the mouth and fold or bag the envelope as the pilot directs.

Practise before you go

  • Read Chapter 6 of the FAA Balloon Flying Handbook (Layout to Launch), especially the crew sections.
  • Practise rope handling and knots, and lifting bulky soft loads with a partner.
  • Get used to 5 am starts in the week before.

Safety notes

  • Leather gloves; never wrap lines around hands; let go if lifted.
  • Dress in natural-fibre layers; sun and eye protection.
  • Respect private land and traffic rules during chase.
