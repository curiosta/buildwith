| Difficulty (our estimate): Beginner (flying a patang well takes some practice); prep time (our estimate): 1 hour reading plus 2–4 short practice sessions on open ground. Difficulty and prep time are Curiosta editorial estimates for a first-timer, not organiser figures. | Curiosta editorial estimate |
| Refresher, practice and safety steps: A patang has no tail and is steered actively: short tugs make it dart or climb, slackening the line lets it drift and rise. Practise small, calm inputs before trying anything fast. / Launch with your back to the wind; let the kite catch the breeze, then give short pulls so it climbs above the rooftop turbulence into steadier wind. / Fly as a pair: one person flies, the other holds the firki and winds or unwinds smoothly so the line never piles up and tangles. / Kite 'fights' (cutting a rival's line) are the heart of Uttarayan; as a first-timer focus on keeping control and enjoy watching the pech battles before joining in. / Buy plain cotton thread. Avoid nylon or synthetic 'Chinese' manja and glass-coated thread: they injure riders, pedestrians and birds, and India's National Green Tribunal directions (2017) prohibit them. / Tape your fingertips or wear finger guards or gloves: a running line can cut or burn skin. / Never stand on parapets or unrailed edges, never chase a falling kite across roofs or roads, and if a kite tangles in power lines, let it go. / Expect birds in the sky at dawn and dusk; fly gently and report any injured bird to a local bird-rescue helpline. / Fly any cheap patang on an open ground or beach in India in the weeks before (they are sold widely around Makar Sankranti); practise tug-and-release steering at ground level, not on a roof. / Practise winding and unwinding a firki with a friend so hand-overs are smooth. / Try a simple single-line kite first if you have never flown one, using the AKA launch steps, then move to a patang. / Make finger guards from medical tape and practise letting line run through gloved fingers. / Use plain cotton thread only; no nylon, synthetic or glass-coated manja. / Protect fingers from line cuts; wear gloves or tape. / Stay off parapets and roof edges; never chase falling kites into traffic. / Keep clear of power lines; abandon a tangled kite. / Fly gently around birds; report injured birds to a local bird-rescue helpline. | Our summary of the resources below |

## Check notes shown on the page

- Use plain cotton thread. India's National Green Tribunal (judgment of 11 Jul 2017) dealt with nylon, synthetic and glass-coated 'Chinese' manja; its directions prohibit nylon thread and thread coated with glass or other harmful substances.
- CONFLICT: the official International Kite Festival 'About' page still describes traditional glass-coated manja. That conflicts with the glass-manja ban, so we advise against glass-coated manja.
- The official International Kite Festival 2027 dates are NOT announced yet; 14 Jan 2027 is Makar Sankranti in the Government of India's 2027 holiday list.
