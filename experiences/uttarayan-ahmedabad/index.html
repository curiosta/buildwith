03 · Skills you'll need

Skills you'll need

What to learn and practise before you go. Each resource is marked Official or Community-accepted and names its publisher. Difficulty and prep time are our estimate.

Core skill

Flying a patang (Indian fighter kite) from a rooftop: launching, steering by tug-and-release, letting out line

Also useful:

  • Working the firki (reel) with a partner and handling manja without cutting yourself
  • Rooftop, street and bird safety during Uttarayan
Difficulty our estimateBeginner (flying a patang well takes some practice)
Prep time our estimate1 hour reading plus 2–4 short practice sessions on open ground

Difficulty and prep time are Curiosta editorial estimates for a first-timer, not organiser figures.

Refresher

  1. A patang has no tail and is steered actively: short tugs make it dart or climb, slackening the line lets it drift and rise. Practise small, calm inputs before trying anything fast.
  2. Launch with your back to the wind; let the kite catch the breeze, then give short pulls so it climbs above the rooftop turbulence into steadier wind.
  3. Fly as a pair: one person flies, the other holds the firki and winds or unwinds smoothly so the line never piles up and tangles.
  4. Kite 'fights' (cutting a rival's line) are the heart of Uttarayan; as a first-timer focus on keeping control and enjoy watching the pech battles before joining in.
  5. Buy plain cotton thread. Avoid nylon or synthetic 'Chinese' manja and glass-coated thread: they injure riders, pedestrians and birds, and India's National Green Tribunal directions (2017) prohibit them.
  6. Tape your fingertips or wear finger guards or gloves: a running line can cut or burn skin.
  7. Never stand on parapets or unrailed edges, never chase a falling kite across roofs or roads, and if a kite tangles in power lines, let it go.
  8. Expect birds in the sky at dawn and dusk; fly gently and report any injured bird to a local bird-rescue helpline.

Practise before you go

  • Fly any cheap patang on an open ground or beach in India in the weeks before (they are sold widely around Makar Sankranti); practise tug-and-release steering at ground level, not on a roof.
  • Practise winding and unwinding a firki with a friend so hand-overs are smooth.
  • Try a simple single-line kite first if you have never flown one, using the AKA launch steps, then move to a patang.
  • Make finger guards from medical tape and practise letting line run through gloved fingers.

Safety notes

  • Use plain cotton thread only; no nylon, synthetic or glass-coated manja.
  • Protect fingers from line cuts; wear gloves or tape.
  • Stay off parapets and roof edges; never chase falling kites into traffic.
  • Keep clear of power lines; abandon a tangled kite.
  • Fly gently around birds; report injured birds to a local bird-rescue helpline.

04 · Check before you go

What we could and couldn't confirm

Before you book

  • Use plain cotton thread. India's National Green Tribunal (judgment of 11 Jul 2017) dealt with nylon, synthetic and glass-coated 'Chinese' manja; its directions prohibit nylon thread and thread coated with glass or other harmful substances.
  • CONFLICT: the official International Kite Festival 'About' page still describes traditional glass-coated manja. That conflicts with the glass-manja ban, so we advise against glass-coated manja.
  • International Kite Festival 2027: 10–14 Jan 2027 at Sabarmati Riverfront, Ahmedabad (Incredible India, Ministry of Tourism, source: Gujarat Tourism). 14 Jan 2027 is Makar Sankranti in the Government of India's 2027 holiday list.
  • Costs are not listed on buildwith. Check them, and the latest dates, on the official site.
