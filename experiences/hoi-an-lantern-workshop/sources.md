| Skills (this experience): Making a Hoi An lantern: shaping a bamboo-rib frame and covering it with silk; Gluing, stretching and trimming fabric neatly over curves; Folding and packing a collapsible lantern to carry home | Skills research, checked 2026-10-04 (IST) |
| Difficulty (our estimate): Beginner; prep time (our estimate): 30 minutes reading; optional 1 practice session. Difficulty and prep time are Curiosta editorial estimates for a first-timer, not organiser figures. | Curiosta editorial estimate |
| Refresher, practice and safety steps: A traditional Hoi An lantern has two stages: the bamboo frame, then the fabric covering. The bamboo is soaked, dried and split into thin ribs, which are fixed between two rings. / Adjust the ribs by eye until the shape is even before any fabric goes on; a lopsided frame shows once lit. / Cut fabric panels to the frame, apply glue sparingly along the ribs, and stretch the silk taut without tearing it. Silk and satin are used because they stretch without ripping. / Trim excess fabric with sharp scissors close to the rib, then add the top grip and tassel. / Class length varies by workshop. Often the artisan prepares the frame and you focus on covering and finishing; ask how to fold it flat for travel. / Light only with an LED or low-heat bulb at home; open flames and silk don't mix. / Make a simple paper lantern on a wire or bamboo-skewer frame at home to practise stretching and gluing over curves. / Practise cutting neat curved edges with fabric scissors on scrap cloth. / Read the provincial tourism department's story on the two-stage process before the class. / Sharp scissors and craft knives: cut away from your body. / Use LED lights in finished silk lanterns, never candles. | Our summary of the resources below |
