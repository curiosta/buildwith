01 · How to take part

Your way in

Make a lantern at one of Hoi An's lantern workshops, then walk the lantern-lit old town on the 14th night of the lunar month.

Workshop / open build

Make a bamboo-and-silk lantern

Hoi An has more than 30 lantern workshops (provincial tourism department). Make a lantern with a local artisan, then walk the old town on Hội An Legendary Night, held on the 14th night of every lunar month, when lanterns replace the street lights. Class times and booking vary by workshop: we don't link a single operator.

03 · Skills you'll need

Skills you'll need

What to learn and practise before you go. Each resource is marked Official or Community-accepted and names its publisher. Difficulty and prep time are our estimate.

Core skill

Making a Hoi An lantern: shaping a bamboo-rib frame and covering it with silk

Also useful:

  • Gluing, stretching and trimming fabric neatly over curves
  • Folding and packing a collapsible lantern to carry home
Difficulty our estimateBeginner
Prep time our estimate30 minutes reading; optional 1 practice session

Difficulty and prep time are Curiosta editorial estimates for a first-timer, not organiser figures.

Refresher

  1. A traditional Hoi An lantern has two stages: the bamboo frame, then the fabric covering. The bamboo is soaked, dried and split into thin ribs, which are fixed between two rings.
  2. Adjust the ribs by eye until the shape is even before any fabric goes on; a lopsided frame shows once lit.
  3. Cut fabric panels to the frame, apply glue sparingly along the ribs, and stretch the silk taut without tearing it. Silk and satin are used because they stretch without ripping.
  4. Trim excess fabric with sharp scissors close to the rib, then add the top grip and tassel.
  5. Class length varies by workshop. Often the artisan prepares the frame and you focus on covering and finishing; ask how to fold it flat for travel.
  6. Light only with an LED or low-heat bulb at home; open flames and silk don't mix.

Practise before you go

  • Make a simple paper lantern on a wire or bamboo-skewer frame at home to practise stretching and gluing over curves.
  • Practise cutting neat curved edges with fabric scissors on scrap cloth.
  • Read the provincial tourism department's story on the two-stage process before the class.

Safety notes

  • Sharp scissors and craft knives: cut away from your body.
  • Use LED lights in finished silk lanterns, never candles.
