## Skills you'll need (section 03)

| Skills (this experience): Snow carving: blocking out a form from a packed snow block using hand tools; Team planning: scale model (maquette), cut plan and division of work; Working safely for long hours in extreme cold | Skills research, checked 2026-10-04 (IST) |
| Difficulty (our estimate): Skilled team (qualified sculptors only); prep time (our estimate): several months of team design and carving practice. Difficulty and prep time are Curiosta editorial estimates for a first-timer, not organiser figures. | Curiosta editorial estimate |
| Refresher, practice and safety steps: Start with a small scale model (in clay or plasticine) of your design and work out how it fits the block from every side. / Mark key points on the block, then remove big masses first, working from the top down so debris falls away from finished areas. / Design for snow: avoid thin, unsupported parts; keep weight over a solid base; snow is strong in compression, weak in overhangs. / Use hand tools (saws, shovels, chisels, rasps, scrapers); peer competitions such as Breckenridge allow hand tools only and ban power tools. / Refine and add texture last; spray or smooth only as the rules allow. / Cold safety: dress in layers, protect hands and feet, take warm breaks, drink water, and use scaffolds and ladders carefully on icy footing. / Carve a design into a block of packed sand, soap or floral foam at home to rehearse removing masses top-down. / Build a small snow or crushed-ice test piece if you can reach a snowfield (e.g. Gulmarg or Himachal in winter). / Make the maquette and a cut plan with your team long before the trip. / Frostbite and hypothermia risk: layers, insulated gloves, regular warm breaks. / Icy scaffolds and ladders: three points of contact, secure footing. / Saws and chisels: keep blades guarded when not in use. | Our summary of the resources below |
