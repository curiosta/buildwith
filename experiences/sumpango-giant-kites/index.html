Skills you'll need

What to learn and practise before you go. Each resource is marked Official or Community-accepted and names its publisher. Difficulty and prep time are our estimate.

Core skill

Understanding giant-kite (barrilete) construction: tissue-paper canvas, bamboo frame, fringes, tail and 'brakes'

Also useful:

  • Flying your own kite (public flying at the festival: Unconfirmed: no official source)
  • Crowd safety near giant kite launches
Difficulty our estimateBeginner (spectator plus your own kite)
Prep time our estimate1 hour reading; optional kite-making evening

Difficulty and prep time are Curiosta editorial estimates for a first-timer, not organiser figures.

Refresher

  1. Barrilete groups work from July to 31 October: they agree a design, scale it up, make the canvas from coloured tissue paper (papel de china), add a lined edge with cord, then lash it to a bamboo (caña de castilla) frame overnight on 31 Oct–1 Nov.
  2. Fringes and a tail give stability, and rope 'brakes' help control flight; giants (2–22 m) may be exhibited rather than flown.
  3. The kites carry community messages and honour the ancestors on All Saints' Day; treat the field and the nearby cemetery with respect.
  4. Watch launches from behind the marked lines: big kites can fall unpredictably.
  5. For your own kite: open space, back to the wind, gloves for strong pull, and never fly near the giant-kite area or power lines.

Practise before you go

  • Make a small hexagonal tissue-paper-and-bamboo kite at home to understand the technique.
  • Fly a simple kite in a park in gusty wind to practise control.
  • Read the Ministry of Culture's description of the technique (Spanish).

Safety notes

  • Stay behind lines at giant-kite launches.
  • Large crowds; plan transport and meeting points.
  • Respect the cemetery and the ceremonies on 1 November.
