| Difficulty (our estimate): Beginner (spectator plus your own kite); prep time (our estimate): 1 hour reading; optional kite-making evening. Difficulty and prep time are Curiosta editorial estimates for a first-timer, not organiser figures. | Curiosta editorial estimate |
| Refresher, practice and safety steps: Barrilete groups work from July to 31 October: they agree a design, scale it up, make the canvas from coloured tissue paper (papel de china), add a lined edge with cord, then lash it to a bamboo (caña de castilla) frame overnight on 31 Oct–1 Nov. / Fringes and a tail give stability, and rope 'brakes' help control flight; giants (2–22 m) may be exhibited rather than flown. / The kites carry community messages and honour the ancestors on All Saints' Day; treat the field and the nearby cemetery with respect. / Watch launches from behind the marked lines: big kites can fall unpredictably. / For your own kite: open space, back to the wind, gloves for strong pull, and never fly near the giant-kite area or power lines. / Make a small hexagonal tissue-paper-and-bamboo kite at home to understand the technique. / Fly a simple kite in a park in gusty wind to practise control. / Read the Ministry of Culture's description of the technique (Spanish). / Stay behind lines at giant-kite launches. / Large crowds; plan transport and meeting points. / Respect the cemetery and the ceremonies on 1 November. | Our summary of the resources below |
