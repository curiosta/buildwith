Refresher

  1. Fly in steady, moderate wind in open space, away from trees, roads, power lines and airports.
  2. Back to the wind; hold the kite by the bridle, let line out and pull in as it points up so it climbs; a helper can launch from 50 ft downwind in light wind.
  3. Sinking tail-first means too little wind; diving or spinning means too much. Move the bridle tow point a little, or add tail for stability.
  4. Walk towards another flier to untangle; never yank.
  5. Gloves for pulling kites; never let line run through bare fingers. Plain cotton thread only at Uttarayan, never nylon or glass-coated manja.
  6. Never fly in storms or near power lines; leave a kite that's tangled in wires.
  7. Build: light but strong spars, a taut sail, a balanced bridle; start from a proven plan before inventing.

Practise before you go

  • Fly a cheap kite on an open ground or beach; practise solo and assisted launches.
  • Build a sled kite from a bin bag and two sticks.
  • Practise untangling with a friend.

Safety notes

  • Power lines: never fly near them; abandon a tangled kite.
  • Line cuts and burns: gloves.
  • No nylon or glass-coated manja.
  • Sun protection; sunburn is the most common kite-field injury (AKA).
