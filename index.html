01 · The idea

Don't just watch the festival. Make your way in.

Every experience here has a hands-on way in. You build something: a lantern, a sand sculpture, a racing bed, a boat made of drink cans. Or you use something that local makers built: a kite from the Patang Bazaar, a samba-school costume, a haneto outfit. For now this is an inspiration site. Each listing explains exactly how a traveller can take part, with links to the official sources.

Build it

Make the thing yourself

Workshops and open builds: lantern classes, clay-idol workshops, theme camps and art at burns, float-building tents.

Use it

Use what locals made

Buy, rent or crew: kites from local makers, samba-school costumes, sky lanterns from Shifen shops, a seat on a balloon chase crew.

Enter it

Race or exhibit your build

Team entries: sand sculpture, bed racing, kinetic sculptures, soapbox karts and cardboard-and-can boats. Some are open, some by application or lottery.

🪁 The founding example: Uttarayan in Ahmedabad

Every January, for Makar Sankranti, Gujarat takes to its rooftops. You can't really join in by watching: you buy kites and reels from the makers at the Patang Bazaar, climb onto a rooftop with a local family and fly. That's the whole idea of buildwith. How to take part in Uttarayan →

Texas SandFest (amateur competition)

Port Aransas, Texas, USA
Team entry

Register solo, as a team or as a family in the amateur categories and build a sand sculpture in your own plot: a 1-day contest on 17 Apr or a 2-day open contest on 16–17 Apr 2027. Registration closes 11 Apr 2027, or earlier if plots fill.

Rio Carnival - parade with a samba school

Sambadrome, Rio de Janeiro, Brazil
Use local gear

Buy a fantasia (costume) made in a samba school's barracão (workshop), from the school or a reseller. The costume is your entry: you parade in one of the school's wings (alas) through the Sambadrome.
